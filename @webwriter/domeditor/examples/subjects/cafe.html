Order a snack in Spanish

Imagine stepping into a small café in Madrid. You want a drink and something to eat, but you are still learning Spanish. This 20-minute lesson gives you a polite order, useful menu words, and a chance to practice a realistic exchange.

Read a café menu

A café menu often groups drinks and food. Look at the original menu below. You will see agua (water), café (coffee), zumo (juice), pan (bread), queso (cheese), and tarta (cake). These are useful words to recognize before you order. In Spanish, café also means the place where you buy a drink, so context helps you understand which meaning is intended.

Put a polite order together

A simple polite request is «Quisiera…, por favor» (“I would like…, please”). You can add an item: «Quisiera un zumo y un bocadillo de queso, por favor». A bocadillo is a sandwich, often made with bread and a filling. To ask the price, say «¿Cuánto cuesta?» (“How much does it cost?”). Listen for «Son cinco euros» (“That is five euros”).

Practice the conversation

Read the dialogue once for the overall meaning. Read it again aloud, taking both roles. Notice the polite greeting «Buenos días» and the closing «Muchas gracias». Spanish often leaves out a subject pronoun when the verb makes the meaning clear. You can keep your request short and courteous without translating each English word directly.

  1. Camarera: Buenos días. ¿Qué desea?
  2. Cliente: Buenos días. Quisiera un zumo y un bocadillo de queso, por favor.
  3. Camarera: Claro. ¿Quiere algo más?
  4. Cliente: No, gracias. ¿Cuánto cuesta?
  5. Camarera: Son cinco euros.
  6. Cliente: Aquí tiene. Muchas gracias.
  7. Camarera: De nada. ¡Que aproveche!
