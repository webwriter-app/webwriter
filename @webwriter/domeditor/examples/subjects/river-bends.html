Why does a river bend?

A river bend is not a fixed shape. Water moves sediment, cuts into one bank, and leaves material along another. Use a map of the Moselle near Bremm and a simple diagram to connect visible shape with a process—while keeping in mind that a map image alone cannot show how fast the channel is changing.

Read a bend as a process

A meander is a winding channel. In a bend, the fastest current tends to swing toward the outside bank. Its force can loosen and carry away material there: erosion. On the inside, water is often slower, so some carried sand and gravel settle: deposition. The pattern is common, but each reach depends on its slope, sediment, vegetation, flood history, and human changes. It is a useful explanation, not a promise that every bend has identical flow.
