Who eats what in the garden?

A garden food chain connects a plant and animals through feeding. Explore the organisms, listen to a robin, and use evidence to explain what each link means.

Start with a food chain

A simple garden example is:

Grass is a producer: it makes its own food using sunlight. A grasshopper that eats plants is a consumer. A robin may eat insects such as grasshoppers. In a real garden, an animal’s diet depends on what is available, so treat this short chain as a model of one possible feeding path.

Observe, listen, explain

Observe

Look closely at the two identified animals. What features help you recognize the grasshopper and the European robin? What can a photograph show, and what can’t it prove about an animal’s diet?

Listen

Play this short recording of a European robin singing. Listen for repeated phrases, changes in pitch, or pauses. The song helps identify a bird; by itself, it does not tell us what the bird ate.

Try it: Listen once with your eyes closed, then once while watching the image. What did you notice each time?

Explain

Use the chain to connect what you observed with the feeding relationship. Grass is the producer; the grasshopper and robin are consumers. The arrow points from the food toward the organism that eats it, so the grasshopper gets food and energy from grass.

Make a prediction: If fewer grasshoppers were available, which organism in this short chain might have less food? Explain why, then name one observation that could help check your idea.

A food web combines many connected chains. One photograph or birdsong recording can help you observe an organism, but evidence about feeding comes from observations of feeding or reliable diet records.
