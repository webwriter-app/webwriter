Who gets space on our street?

Imagine a fictional town, Lindenfeld, deciding how to redesign Market Street. The street is shared by people with different needs: a bus rider, a child who cycles, a shop owner receiving deliveries, and a resident who walks with a cane. Explore three proposals and notice who gains time, safety, or access—and who may face a new cost. There is no single correct allocation.

A street is a public choice

Street space is limited. A wider bus lane may make trips more reliable, while reducing room for parking or deliveries. A protected cycle lane can make cycling feel safer, yet requires space and careful junction design. Wider pavements help people walking, especially those using mobility aids, but can affect loading or vehicle access. These are trade-offs about access, safety, time, and public priorities. The example is fictional; its people and numbers are invented for discussion.

Choose a proposal and follow its effects

Start the scenario and choose one of three plans for Lindenfeld’s Market Street. Each option changes how the same limited width is used: prioritize walking, prioritize buses, or create a protected cycle route with timed delivery access. Read the stakeholder responses, then compare the consequences. The scenario does not score choices as right or wrong. Ask whose needs were represented, which were missing, and what evidence the town should gather before building.

Market Street meeting

Lindenfeld has one narrow street. Choose the priority you want the council to test first. Your choice changes who gets space and what needs further planning.

Each option is a proposal for discussion, not a scored answer.

Walking and waiting

Widen pavements and add a resting place near the bus stop. Sam may find the route easier; Mara may still face delay if buses have no priority. Nina needs a planned loading window. What would make the crossings accessible?

More reliable buses

Give buses a dedicated lane and clear boarding space. Mara may save time; curbside loading becomes harder and the cycle route still needs protection at junctions. Where could deliveries stop without blocking the pavement?

Protected cycling and timed deliveries

Protect a cycle route and reserve delivery access at set times. Ivo may feel safer; buses may need careful stop design and Sam needs an uncluttered pavement. Which junction should the town study first?

If you had to revise your chosen plan after hearing from someone affected, what would you change? How could residents with different schedules, ages, and mobility needs take part in the decision?
