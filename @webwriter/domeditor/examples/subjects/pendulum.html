Measuring the Acceleration Due to Gravity in Your Living Room

Tasks:

1) Use the simulation and measure the period of a pendulum with the stopwatch, keeping the mass the same and using a fixed amplitude between 5° and 10° (one angle for all measurements). Vary the length of the pendulum from one measurement to the next. Enter the values in the table below.

2) Use your calculator to square your times and enter these too. Then switch to the "Scatter Plot" tab of the table and plot T² against the length l. Then draw a straight line through all the points and use its gradient m for the calculation. 
 

The acceleration due to gravity describes how strongly objects are accelerated by the Earth’s gravitational pull. At the Earth’s surface it is about 9.81 m/s². With the help of a pendulum, you can determine this value experimentally without having to measure the acceleration directly.

To do this, you investigate how the period T, i.e. the time for one complete oscillation, depends on the length of the pendulum l. For small angles of deflection, the following applies to an ideal pendulum: T = 2π l g . The longer the pendulum, the longer the period. The mass of the pendulum bob, on the other hand, has no effect on the period.

To investigate the relationship on a graph, the equation is squared: T2 = 4π2 g · l . If you plot the squared periods against the corresponding pendulum lengths, you get an approximately straight line. Its gradient m describes how much the square of the period increases as the length of the pendulum increases.

By comparing this with the general equation of a straight line y=m·x , you can see that the gradient is given by: m = 4π2 g . Rearranging this equation to make the acceleration due to gravity the subject gives: g = 4π2 m . This means you can use the gradient of the line of best fit to calculate the acceleration due to gravity. You can then compare your result with the known value of about 9.81 m/s².
