Eat and be eaten?

Food chains in our garden

An apple tree. As every autumn, it is once again bearing so much delicious fruit that you can hardly keep up with picking it all. But how does the tree actually manage to grow all those apples year after year? 

Clearly, it can’t do this just like that. To do so, it needs two things: nutrients and energy.
The nutrients come from the soil and are taken up through the roots. The energy comes from the sun and is made usable for the tree through photosynthesis. 

But what about the apples? Some of the energy and nutrients are stored in them. Other living things can use this – us, for example :)

Because the tree itself does not consume any other living things or parts of them, it is called a producer.Have you ever found a caterpillar in an apple? It’s annoying, but actually just as it should be – after all, a caterpillar has to live on something. An apple, for example. But we also find other caterpillars on the tree’s leaves, which are apparently tasty too. 

Because caterpillars feed on other living things, we call them consumers, and because the caterpillar only consumes producers, it is a primary consumer. 
But there are other living things in a garden too, including some that like to eat caterpillars. A tit, for example. That makes it one of the secondary consumers.
But even the tit is not safe from larger animals, such as cats. So cats, in the end, are tertiary consumers.

Within this food chain, energy is passed on from one living thing to the next. But not much of it: usually less than 10% at each step.








The decomposers are nowhere near as grim as their name might sound. In fact, they do an important job and make sure that the other living things can go on existing in the future:

When apples fall to the ground, when animals need to relieve themselves, or when they eventually die, earthworms, woodlice, fungi and others make sure that the remains in particular can get back into the soil as usable nutrients. Of course, they use up energy in the process too. Unlike consumers, however, they do not feed directly on other living things.
