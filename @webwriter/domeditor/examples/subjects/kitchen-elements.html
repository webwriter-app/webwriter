What is a kitchen made of?

A kitchen looks like one place, but its objects contain many kinds of matter. Investigate three familiar materials and use the periodic table to distinguish an element from a mixture or a compound.

From objects to materials

A sheet of kitchen foil is usually mostly aluminium (Al), though commercial foil can contain small amounts of other substances. Electrical wire is often copper (Cu), sometimes covered by insulation or a thin coating. The dark line in a pencil is graphite: a form of carbon (C), mixed with clay and held in wood. These objects are materials, not tiny pieces of pure element in every case.

Explore the periodic table. Select Al, Cu, and C and note each symbol and name. The table describes chemical elements: substances whose atoms have the same number of protons. It does not list every material or object. A copper atom and a carbon atom are different kinds of atoms, even when both occur in the same kitchen.

Sort the ideas carefully

An element contains one kind of atom. A compound contains atoms of different elements chemically joined in a fixed ratio, as in water (H₂O). A mixture combines substances without joining every particle into one new compound; air and many alloys are mixtures. Graphite is elemental carbon, but pencil “lead” is a mixture of graphite and clay. Foil and wire are useful everyday materials, but their exact composition depends on manufacture.
