What changes a swing’s rhythm?

A pendulum swings back and forth in a repeating rhythm. Use a simulation to investigate what changes its period, the time for one complete swing. You will compare one factor at a time, record evidence, and reflect on what the model can tell you. Allow about 20 minutes.

A model you can change

A pendulum’s period depends strongly on its length. For small swings, changing the bob’s mass does not change the period much, while increasing the length makes each cycle take longer. This is an approximation for small angles: large starting angles can change the period slightly. The simulation is a model, so use controlled comparisons rather than treating one run as proof about every real swing.

Change one factor

First compare two masses while keeping length and starting angle fixed. Then compare two lengths while keeping mass and angle fixed. Record your settings and measured time in the table. A fair test changes one variable at a time: if length and mass both change, you cannot tell which change caused a difference. Timing ten cycles reduces the effect of reaction-time error on a single cycle.

Investigate and compare

Use the second tab to inspect a pendulum diagram. The bob’s path repeats; one period runs from a chosen point in the motion back to that same point moving in the same direction. In your measurements, note small differences between trials rather than rounding them away. If the mass comparison is nearly unchanged, report that result; do not claim that mass can never matter under any conditions.
