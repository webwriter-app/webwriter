A river town decides

Your mission

You are a citizens’ panel in a fictional river town. Recommend how to spend a budget of 100 planning units. Compare competing priorities and explain opportunity cost. These invented costs are for classroom debate; they are not engineering estimates or a plan for the real place on the map.

1. Read a river landscape

The map is centred on the Rhine near Mainz, Germany, as a real example of a river crossing an urban area. Locate a bridge, riverbank, and built-up area. Use them to generate questions a fictional town would need to investigate; a base map alone cannot establish flood risk.

Without map tiles: Imagine homes on the west bank, a station on the east bank, one connecting bridge, and a riverside park. Discuss whose access depends on the bridge. All budget work below can be completed using this description.

2. Compare the proposals

Fictional one-off costs; choose whole projects within 100 units
Project Cost Stated aim
A · Riverside wetland 45 Habitat and water storage; needs space
B · Raised walking route 35 More reliable pedestrian access
C · Warning and outreach 20 Accessible information and practice drills
D · Community shelter upgrade 50 Improve a shared building and access

Resident perspective

“Information must reach people without a smartphone and people who use different languages.”

Ask: how will you check whether the outreach is accessible?

Business perspective

“We depend on people being able to cross the river, but construction may disrupt us.”

Ask: who experiences the benefits and the temporary costs?

Ecology perspective

“A wetland could provide habitat as well as water storage, but the location and design matter.”

Ask: what site-specific evidence would be needed before making a claim about its effect?

3. Deliberate and justify

In groups of four, assign chair, budget checker, accessibility advocate, and evidence checker. Compare at least two affordable combinations. Agree on one recommendation and name a valuable project it excludes.

Exit ticket: State one reason a reasonable panel could choose differently.
