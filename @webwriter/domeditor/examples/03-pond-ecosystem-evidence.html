Reading a School Pond Ecosystem

Reasoning toolkit

Energy and matter

Food-web arrows point from food to consumer. Algae → water fleas → small fish.

For a second simplified chain, use duckweed → pond snails → small fish. Energy flows through the web; nutrients cycle through living and non-living parts.

Evidence and explanation

Two zones are a starting point, not a replicated experiment.

Look for differences in light, temperature, fish, and sampling time before deciding what caused an oxygen difference.

Investigate the scenario

A school pond team sampled two similar shallow zones in the same week. Zone A was shaded by reeds; Zone B received more afternoon light. The following results are fictional paired samples, measured with the same methods.

Fictional pond samples
Measure Zone A Zone B
Light at surface (lux) 1,200 4,800
Algal cover (%) 18 46
Water fleas (per litre) 32 15
Small fish (count) 6 11
Dissolved oxygen (mg/L) 8.1 6.4

A simplified food web is: light → algae → water fleas → small fish. Algae are producers; water fleas and fish are consumers. Decomposers break down dead material and return nutrients. Zone B has more light and algae, but fewer water fleas, more fish, and lower oxygen. Several variables differ together, so a causal claim needs care. Correlation means two measurements vary together; causation requires a tested mechanism and controlled comparisons. A single snapshot can reveal patterns worth investigating, but it cannot settle the explanation.

  1. Trace energy: Draw arrows from producer to consumer for two chains. Label each organism’s trophic role and add decomposers in a place where they act on dead matter.
  2. Compare patterns: Calculate the algal-cover difference (28 percentage points) and oxygen difference (1.7 mg/L). Describe one positive association and one negative association in the paired data.
  3. Test the explanation: A learner claims “extra light caused the oxygen decrease.” List two confounding possibilities, then design a follow-up: keep light similar, sample at the same time, and measure at least three ponds or zones repeatedly.
