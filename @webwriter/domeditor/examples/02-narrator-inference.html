What the Narrator Leaves Out

Reasoning toolkit

Evidence

Separate what Mara says, what the narrator observes, and what a reader infers.

Use the stem: “The detail … suggests … because …”.

Alternative reading

Test your inference against an alternative explanation.

Use the stem: “However, … could also mean …; the text does not tell us …”.

Investigate the scenario

Read this original short story.

At 4:10, Mara said the auditorium was empty. She pushed the side door and heard one bright note from the piano. “The wind,” she told herself, although the windows were shut. On the stage, a paper crown lay beside a fresh puddle. Mara stepped around it and found the caretaker’s key on the floor. She returned it to the office, certain she had done the sensible thing. At the noticeboard, a new poster announced auditions had moved to the auditorium. Mara folded the poster into her pocket. Later, when Jo asked why her shoes were wet, Mara said she had walked through the garden. Jo looked at the crown, then at Mara’s pocket, and smiled as if an answer had arrived.

The narrator reports some details directly: the time, shut windows, puddle, key, poster, and wet shoes. Other statements are claims or interpretations, such as “the auditorium was empty,” “the sensible thing,” and Jo’s smile “as if an answer had arrived.” These do not have equal certainty.

Claim and evidence comparison
Narrator claim Possible evidence Alternative reading
The auditorium was empty. A piano note and fresh puddle are present. Someone may have left moments earlier.
Mara did the sensible thing. She returned the key. She also put the poster in her pocket; her reason is unclear.
Jo understood. Jo notices the crown and pocket. Jo may only suspect a story.
  1. Annotate: Mark each sentence as observation, claim, or inference. Circle the two details that most strongly challenge Mara’s account.
  2. Compare interpretations: Write one paragraph arguing that Mara entered for a harmless reason and one paragraph arguing that she concealed something. Each paragraph must use two details and a modal word such as might or suggests.
  3. Evaluate voice: Explain how the third-person narrator creates uncertainty by presenting Mara’s thoughts alongside physical clues. Identify one unanswered question.


Check your understanding

The story explicitly proves that Mara stole the caretaker’s key.
