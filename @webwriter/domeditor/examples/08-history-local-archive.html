Whose town is in the archive?

Your mission

You are preparing a museum label about a fictional town called Millhaven. The archive below is invented for source-analysis practice. Build a chronology, compare two viewpoints, and distinguish what a source says from what it can establish.

1. Explore the archive timeline

Open each event. Record one change in transport, work, or public space. The dates refer only to this fictional town.

Railway station opens

A council notice announces a new passenger station and asks residents to keep the approach road clear.

 Market moves to Station Square

A map marks the new market beside the station. It does not record who supported or opposed the move.

 Factory adds a night shift

A company newsletter celebrates longer production hours. No worker interviews survive in this collection.

 Residents request a playground

A petition asks the council for a play area away from busy delivery roads. Signing the petition did not mean every resident agreed.

2. Compare two voices from 1910

“The new square gives traders room to sell and visitors a fine first impression of our town.”

Source A: fictional council publicity leaflet, 1910

“My stall is easier to unload now, but the walk is longer for customers from the old streets.”

Source B: fictional market trader’s diary, 1910
Use these questions to interrogate each source
Question What to look for
Who made it? Role, intended audience, and purpose
What does it claim? Specific words or details
What is missing? Other residents’ experiences and independent evidence

3. Curate the label

Both sources support the claim that every resident benefited equally from the move.
