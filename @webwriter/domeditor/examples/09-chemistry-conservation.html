Account for every atom

Your mission

You are checking a scientific illustrator’s particle diagram. Show that a balanced chemical equation has the same number of each type of atom on both sides. This is a paper-and-screen modelling activity; no substances are mixed.

1. Inspect the proposed equation

The illustrator wrote H2 + O2 → H2O. Count each atom rather than judging whether the equation looks familiar. A subscript belongs to a formula; a coefficient counts complete molecules.

Read the formula

H2O represents two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom in each water molecule.

Changing H2O to H2O2 changes the substance. It is not a way to balance the water equation.

Balance oxygen

H2 + O2 → 2H2O

Now oxygen balances: two atoms on each side. But the right side has four hydrogen atoms and the left has only two.

Balance hydrogen

2H2 + O2 → 2H2O

Both sides now contain four hydrogen atoms and two oxygen atoms. The coefficients are the smallest whole-number ratio 2 : 1 : 2.

2. Audit the final model

Atom inventory for 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
Element Reactants Products
Hydrogen 2 × 2 = 4 2 × 2 = 4
Oxygen 1 × 2 = 2 2 × 1 = 2

Make a particle model with two colours of counters. Rearrange four hydrogen counters and two oxygen counters into two water molecules. Explain why the number of molecules changes even though the number of atoms does not.

3. Transfer the idea

Now balance N2 + H2 → NH3 . Draw or list the atom counts to justify your equation.
