Build a four-beat groove

Your mission

Your group will create an eight-bar body-percussion soundtrack for a school animation. Keep a steady pulse, read simple rhythm patterns, and explain how a rest changes the music. No instrument or recording is needed.

1. Feel the pulse

Count “1, 2, 3, 4” at a comfortable walking pace. Repeat four times. Keep tapping the pulse on your knees while your partner claps a pattern. In this lesson, a quarter note lasts one beat and two eighth notes share one beat.

2. Rehearse three variations

Read each card, practise it four times, and describe what changed. Use a quieter gesture if clapping is uncomfortable.

A · Steady

Clap | clap | clap | clap

Four quarter notes: four sounds across four beats. Keep each beat equally spaced.

B · Space

Clap | rest | clap | rest

Two sounds and two rests still take four beats. Keep silently counting during the rests.

C · Subdivide

Clap | tap-tap | rest | clap

Each quick tap lasts half a beat. This pattern has four sounds, but the time is divided differently from A.

3. Compose and check

Choose A, B, or C for each of eight bars. Give the opening two bars a clear pattern, change something in the middle, and repeat the opening at the end. Write your bar sequence before performing. One learner keeps the pulse and another cues the changes.

Pattern B is shorter in time than pattern A when both use the same pulse.

Complete the duration statement.

Two eighth notes together last one beat.

Peer feedback: Identify one bar where the pulse stayed steady and one transition to rehearse again.
